---
title: How switching decides
description: Engagement, screening bars, pace pressure, and the greedy vs hard paths
---

The decision has two layers: an engagement gate, and then a greedy or hard path.

## Engagement

Below `policy.greedySessionFloor` (default 80, in percent of the 5-hour session window), the decision does not run at all - a fresh session rides its account. Once the active session window is 80% used, or any bar is crossed, the decision engages.

## Screening bars

The thresholds are screening bars, not the trigger for panic:

- `thresholds.session`: a ladder, default `[90]`, a single rung. Each evaluation resolves one active rung: the lowest rung at which some pooled account, the current one included, is still usable. With a multi-rung ladder such as `[50, 80, 95]`, while any account sits under 50, the bar is 50, so a seat that crosses 50 hands off to that account. Once every account is past 50 the bar climbs to 80, and past 80 everywhere it climbs to 95. The top rung is the terminal bar; beyond it the wall (below) takes over. A 5-hour reset is cheap to sit out, so a multi-rung pool drains level by level and no account runs far ahead of its siblings.
- `thresholds.weekly`: 98. This one bar covers both the 7-day aggregate and every per-model weekly cap. Weekly quota is use-it-or-lose-it, so it drains close to the wall.

Both are deliberately below 100: the headroom is the budget to reach a clean turn boundary (plus up to one turn of adoption lag on macOS) before the account actually hits its limit. The same bars screen swap candidates: an account over any of its bars is not a valid target. Trigger and screening always use the same effective bars (the active rung and the weekly bar, each minus `policy.projectionMargin`), because a screen laxer than the trigger would land swaps on accounts the trigger immediately re-flags, ping-ponging the pool. A rung counts only accounts that are usable at it: a dead grant, a burnt gated per-model cap, or a server-pinned enforcement does not hold the bar down, and the rung is re-read from the pool on every retry inside one evaluation. Codex reads the top rung only, because restart is its switch.

## The target: pace pressure

**Pace pressure** is remaining weekly percent divided by time until that account's weekly reset, with higher values preferred and ties broken by soonest reset then lowest weekly usage.

Automatic selection prefers a candidate in the current organization when it has enough measured headroom, then ranks by pace pressure.

| Selection rule | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Organization preference | Requires session usage below `greedySessionFloor` and more than `max(1, session bar - greedySessionFloor)` percentage points below every applicable bar |
| Default screening bars | Preference requires session usage below 80% and aggregate and gated weekly usage below 88% |
| Unknown usage | Receives no organization preference |
| Same organization, insufficient headroom | Competes by pace pressure with the other candidates |
| Manual `tokenmaxxing switch` | Uses pace pressure without organization preference or incumbent margin |

Anthropic documents cache isolation between organizations and, on some API platforms, between workspaces within an organization, so sharing an organization does not guarantee a cache hit. See the [cache documentation](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching#cache-storage-and-sharing) and the [measured profile](/switching-profile).

Before an automatic handoff, the decision attempts to refresh a candidate's usage sample when it is older than `policy.usagePollTtlMs`.

- Parked accounts usually exceed this TTL because there is no periodic parked-account sampler.
- Each evaluation attempts at most two candidates, with one `/usage` attempt per candidate and a 12-second deadline covering identity reads and the CLI.
- Verification skips expiring access tokens and gives the isolated CLI an access token without its refresh grant.
- A successful sample is stored and selection runs again, including pace ranking and the current screening rung.
- Crossing a screening bar does not remove an account from the wall squeeze or reset selection.
- An unavailable sample or exhausted verification budget leaves cached figures in force, so stale-target swaps remain possible.
- An account requiring reauthentication is excluded for the evaluation.

Everything runs off cached windows stored as absolute UTC epochs, so a stale snapshot still resolves correctly: a weekly reset that has passed extrapolates forward in exact 7-day steps, and any window past its cached reset counts as freshly empty (never a reason to switch, never a reason to keep an account benched).

## Greedy vs hard path

- **Greedy path** starts while the current account is usable and permits a lateral move only within its organization.

  `policy.greedySwapMargin`, default 0.15, holds the incumbent unless a positive-pace candidate exceeds its pace pressure by more than that fraction.

  A pool with one account per organization therefore holds until a screening bar or enforced limit triggers the hard path, which can forfeit more unused weekly allowance in exchange for fewer cross-organization handoffs.
- **Hard path** starts when a screening bar is crossed and may cross organizations using the headroom preference above.

  A dead refresh grant is marked for reauthentication and the next candidate is tried.

  If no candidate clears the screening bars, the wall squeeze runs before depleted-wait selection.

  Only the supervised Stop hook may pre-park onto a blocked account while pausing the session.

## The wall

When no candidate clears the screening bars, the decision re-evaluates against the **wall bars**, set by `hardThresholds` and defaulting to 100%.

- A seat below the wall holds and uses its remaining quota.
- A walled seat selects a candidate below the wall using the headroom preference above and bounded verification of stale usage.
- With default bars, remaining candidates have already failed the stricter headroom requirement, so wall selection ranks by pace pressure.
- The pool parks only when every eligible account has reached its wall, while dead refresh grants are set aside for reauthentication.
- Recovery is measured against the wall, allowing an account with a reset session window to run even while its weekly usage remains above the screening bar.

This keeps the last few percent of each account's quota from going unspent. Wall reading uses the same usage source the decision already has. Statusline tee on a supervised session. `/usage` probe on a headless run. Same figures Claude's `/rate-limit-options` shows. A real max-out gets detected and skipped. A single-turn overshoot lands on the next decision. Periodic check, next spawn, or Stop boundary on a headless run.

`hardThresholds` subtracts `policy.projectionMargin` the same way the screening bars do, so setting it equal to `thresholds` disables the wall squeeze. This is a Claude-only path: bare `tokenmaxxing switch` keeps its cache-only park, and Codex ignores `hardThresholds` entirely - a running Codex cannot adopt a swapped credential mid-session, so it stays on its current account until the real server limit (a custom wall below 100 does not apply to Codex).

## Per-model caps

`policy.switchModels` (default `["fable"]`) names the model families whose per-model weekly cap can trigger a switch. Matching is by family token, never by exact display string: model display names drift ("Fable" became "Fable 5"), and an exact-string gate once silently disabled per-model switching entirely. Two related safety rules:

- An unknown active model gates every configured family. Right after a swap clears snapshots, a headless machine can run model-blind; treating unknown as unconstrained once let an account sit at its Fable cap for hours unnoticed.
- Candidate screening counts gated per-model caps too, so a pool where every account has burnt its Fable cap does not round-robin.

## Cooldown

A 45-second post-swap cooldown gates the automatic path. The statusline tee is suppressed for the same 45 seconds after a swap (adoption takes up to 30 seconds on macOS), so an earlier evaluation would run on data the swap itself invalidated - that exact scenario once produced an A-to-B-and-back respawn loop. Manual `tokenmaxxing switch` is unaffected by the cooldown.

## Enforced limits

The cached figures can lag reality: the live account's per-model cap comes only from a `/usage` probe of the busy live token, which often prints nothing under session load. When the server actually refuses a turn, Claude Code ends it on an API error and fires its `StopFailure` hook instead of `Stop`. tokenmaxxing installs that hook with a `rate_limit` matcher, and it reads the transcript's structured error row (the same row Claude Code marks `isApiErrorMessage`): a `quotaLimits` block names the window (`five_hour`, `seven_day`, or a per-model type) with the server's reset, and the Fable cap failure, which carries no `quotaLimits`, is recognized from the client's own error rendering plus the 429 body. Model prose is never read.

The hook stamps the enforced window at 100 for that account and pins a server-dated session or weekly limit on the account until that reset, so an account with no usage sample yet cannot be picked straight back. It hands the enforcement to the decision as a fact stronger than any cached window (it bypasses the post-swap cooldown, gates candidates by the tripped family, and never holds the seat), and the hard path swaps away. In a supervised session whose main turn failed, it also drops a respawn marker: the supervisor relaunches `--resume` on the fresh account and submits one continuation prompt so the interrupted work picks back up. A failure inside a subagent swaps but never respawns (the parent turn is still running), and an unsupervised session gets the swap and adopts it on its next request.

A failure counts only when it provably happened on the post-swap credential: a relaunch after the swap, or a failing request that landed after the swap plus the cooldown. A rate-limit refusal arrives at request start, so the error row's own time is the anchor, however long the turn ran. That is what lets one retrigger sequence cross several burnt accounts without a request still holding an old token stamping the fresh seat.

## Check cadence

The periodic timer runs `tokenmaxxing check --if-due` once per tick (`policy.checkIntervalMs`, 60 seconds by default), and each evaluation schedules the next one, counted from the tick's start, from the live account's headroom to its nearest bar (the active session rung, weekly aggregate, and every gated per-model cap): 40 points or more of headroom sleeps five ticks, 20 sleeps three, 8 sleeps two, and less than that evaluates every tick. The ladder also caps that sleep one band per rung climbed: on the first rung up to five ticks, on the second up to three, on the third up to two, and any rung past that every tick, so a pool that has climbed a level is polled more often than one still on its first rung. A tick landing within half a tick of the due time counts as due, so a slow evaluation cannot push a band out by a whole tick. A bare `tokenmaxxing check` ignores the schedule and evaluates at once. A tee older than the poll TTL, or no tee for the live org, keeps the three-tick cadence, and a gated family with no cap row never sleeps past two ticks, so an unmeasured window can never buy the slow lane. A depleted wait sleeps toward its reset, a tick inside the post-swap cooldown sleeps exactly to its end, and a cap row older than the poll TTL counts as unmeasured. A swap clears the schedule so the fresh seat is evaluated as soon as the cooldown lifts. The slow lane is safe only because the StopFailure hook catches enforcement the moment it happens.
