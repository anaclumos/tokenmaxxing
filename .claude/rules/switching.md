# Switching

One rule: under both bars the seat holds; at or over a bar, `pickBest` ranks the usable candidates and the session moves by respawn under the winner's store; with nothing usable, the depleted-wait path queues first come first served onto a reset target (`pickWaitTarget`, at most 8 waiters per account in `wait-queue.json`; overflow waits for the next reset with room up to twice `policy.maxWaitMs` out, joining the least-crowded reset only when the horizon is full) and resumes on that reset. The same ranking places a session at launch (`pickSeat`); a launch onto a depleted pool takes the same target selection, counting running sessions as well as queued waiters. The Claude supervisor's launch gate (`runSupervisor`, under the pool lock, after `resolveLaunch`) is the one place that acts on a depleted pool: it spawns nothing, claims the target's reset in `wait-queue.json`, counts down to it, and resolves again; a respawn marker whose `waitUntil` is in the future (a depleted wait) skips the compaction, clears the marker's account, and goes through that gate. A pi launch starts immediately. Claude ranking: `seatHeadroom`, `(session bar - session used) / (live sessions + 1)`, highest first, then `pacePressure` (remaining percent of the binding gated window, or the weekly aggregate when the account has no gated row, over time to its reset, highest first), then soonest reset, then lowest weekly use, then fewest live sessions (the only key that separates accounts with no cached figure); unknown usage last. `seatHeadroom` under `MIN_SEAT_HEADROOM` (5) is a placement screen (`seatFull`, only when `PickCtx.seats` is set, never on an account with no session window or one that spends credits, and only while the bar at the session window's end over the same session count clears the floor, so a reset can lift it): `pickBest` skips the account, `usableAt` evaluates the screen at the time its bars stop blocking (a release time included) and then holds it until its session window ends, and an unnamed `seat` borrow screens through `pickBest` while a named one does not, so a burst of launches or moves spills to the next account, a key, or a reset instead of filling one account that then moves every session at once. It never enters `isExhausted`, so it never triggers a move off a full seat. Codex ranks by `pacePressure`, then soonest weekly reset, then lowest weekly use, excludes accounts present in another codex session, and has no manual switch either; neither pool has one. Policy lives in `docs/content/docs/switching.mdx`. The supervisor side of a move (marker, relaunch, relay, seat watch) is in `supervisor.md`.

- One engine, two providers: `decide.ts`, `picker.ts`, `status`, and the account commands take a `Provider` (`src/lib/provider.ts`) and never branch on its name. What differs per product lives in `src/lib/claude.ts` and `src/lib/codex.ts` (identity, credential store, usage source, presence, login and install flows, the swap) and in the codex entries (restart on swap). A product-only rule is a provider capability (`seats`: `shared` for Claude, `live` for Codex, which decides whether present accounts stay candidates, whether `PickCtx.seats` carries the session counts, and whether a move needs the caller's respawn; `waitsWhenDepleted`; `presence`; `gatedFamilies` returning `null` for "every named limit gates"), not an `if` in the engine. Every cached window carries its own duration and sample time, so `isSessionWindow` is one duration test for both providers.
- Window merging is a provider fact: the Claude tee and a Claude sample can omit per-model rows that still apply, so the Claude adapter retains them; a codex usage response is complete, so an empty limit list replaces the stored rows. The engine and the sampler call `p.mergeWindows` and never merge on their own.
- A provider's live observation takes its own pool lock when it may rewrite a credential (codex `observeLive` with `probe: true`) and never locks when the engine already holds the lock (`probe: false`): a second `flock` on a fresh descriptor blocks the same process.
- `sampleOldest` probes accounts with a live session first, then the oldest, so a seat's cached figure is about one sample interval old instead of a full round of the pool. `sampleIntervalMs` in `sample.ts` is the one cadence gate for the tick, `status`, and the in-decision probe: 15 minutes times the unused fraction of the bar closest to being reached, never under `usagePollTtlMs`; 15 minutes for an account at or over an aggregate bar, which only a reset changes, and for an account with no presence record on this host, which only a reset or another host moves; `usagePollTtlMs` for an unmeasured account. A `switchModels` cap already at its bar drops out of the computation, because it screens only that family's sessions. The three recurring samplers (the tick, `status`, the in-decision probe) claim each read by stamping `lastProbeAt` under the pool lock before they send it (the tick in its reservation, the other two through `claimSample`, which rechecks the stored record), so concurrent Stop hooks and `status` runs send an account one read. The in-decision probe gates on `lastProbeAt` alone, and so does the tick for a live account that carries a gated per-model row: a tee fold moves `lastUsageAt` every turn but carries no per-model rows, so gating the probe on `lastUsageAt` would stop a Fable seat's cap from refreshing.
- Build EVERY Claude PickCtx and trigger from `thresholdBars(cfg)`: `thresholds.session` minus `policy.projectionMargin`, and `thresholds.weekly` as configured (the margin guards only the session window against single-turn overshoot; a weekly margin would strand use-it-or-lose-it headroom). Trigger and screening share the bars, or a move lands on an account the trigger re-flags and ping-pongs. Codex reads the same bars. Screening exhaustion is scoped to its bar and never enters the `rejected` set. A bar belongs to an account and a moment: compare a window only through `barFor(account, window, bars, now)`, never against `bars.session` or `bars.weekly`, because a `thresholds.accounts` entry replaces both for that label until the window is within `policy.accountReleaseMs` of its reset, and then the higher of the two holds. The session ladder, `hardThresholds`, the engagement floor, organization affinity, incumbent hysteresis, and the wall squeeze are deleted; do not reintroduce a second bar per window.
- `isExhausted` is the one screen: session, weekly aggregate, gated per-model caps, and `enforcedUntil`, each against its bar. The depleted pause happens at the bar, not at 100. A `thresholds.accounts` entry with `credits: true` skips every window in the screen (`spendsCredits` in `picker.ts`); a session on it past a bar (`onCredits`) counts as over in `isOver`, and the pick moves it only to an account that is not `onCredits` and has not refused the session (`EvalOpts.refused`, from `TOKENMAXXING_REFUSED` in the Stop and SessionStart hooks and from the supervisor's list in the seat watch, or a refusing plan account and the credits seat restart the session in turn) and otherwise returns `no-plan-headroom` before the depleted path, while the Claude and pi seat watches judge the seat with `hasCredits: false`; a Codex read whose `credits.has_credits` is false (`Account.hasCredits`) puts the account back under its bars, while Claude has no credit-status read. `pickBest` ranks such an account last while it would be exhausted without the flag (`onCredits`), and `borrowCodexSeat` drops it before its fewest-borrowers filter whenever another account has plan headroom, so credits pay only when no account has plan headroom. `landWindows` judges an early reset with the flag off. A `quotaLimits` row whose `rateLimitType` is `overage` (the binary's "usage credit limit") classifies as `kind: overage`, walled until the plan answers again (`enforcedWall`: the cached weekly reset when the cached weekly window is at 100, else the session reset), never at the row's reset, which can be the credit cycle's.
- Fresh windows land in an account record through `landWindows` in `picker.ts` and nowhere else (a tee fold, a direct read, the decision's fold, the `status` merge, the hub's in-memory fold). Every site that saves runs under the pool lock, the lock the StopFailure stamp and pick hold, so a clear never interleaves with a stamp. It is the one other writer of `enforcedUntil`: it drops the wall when the new session and weekly aggregates are under their bars while the previous ones blocked before their reset (an early server-side reset, such as a manual one); a window whose reset has passed already reads 0 in the previous figures, so a scheduled reset never clears a wall. It never consults per-model rows, because a tee carries none. The wall does not record what it stands for, so the rule is a proxy: a wall stamped while neither aggregate blocked holds to its deadline, and one stamped while an aggregate blocked, a per-model wall included, also drops at that aggregate's early reset, at the cost of at most one refused turn per early reset.
- Selection reads cached figures only, after folding every account's tee under the lock (`teeObservation`/`foldTee` in `sample.ts`; ordering between a tee and the record uses the sample time inside the tee, never the file mtime, because Linux stamps mtime from the coarse clock and it can lag `Date.now()` by a tick). Freshness comes from the check tick: `sampleOldest` samples up to three accounts per tick, accounts with a live session first and then the ones whose last attempt (`lastUsageAt` or `lastProbeAt`) is oldest, skipping any attempted within its sample interval, over the direct usage read with each account's stored token. A failed attempt is recorded too, so a broken account does not starve the rest of the round-robin, and its cached figure stays as stale as it was; the failure stays visible in the log and never proves a target safe. Five consecutive store-read failures stamp `needsReauth` (a missing credential never recovers on its own); a successful read resets the counter. The pool lock covers only the reservation (`prepareSample`: a store read, no network) and the result write; the usage GET (`runSample`) runs outside the lock so the StopFailure stamp and move never wait on it. Do not move it back under the lock. The read gets one attempt per sample on every path (the round-robin is the retry loop), and its windows are stored only when it is newer than `lastUsageAt`; its banked reset grant lands on its own clock (see the banked reset rule below). There is no post-swap cooldown on either pool.
- Codex has no depleted pause: each session moves by its own respawn, so there is nothing to pause, and a fully depleted pool rides each account until the server refuses it.
- The periodic timer runs a plain `check` every `policy.checkIntervalMs` tick, the tick `init` writes into the timer unit (a changed tick needs `init` re-run; the Nix `intervalSeconds` option is kept equal by hand). The self-paced cadence (`--if-due`, `nextcheck.json`, headroom bands) is deleted and is not to return. A tick has no seat, so it never moves anything; it folds tees, claims banked resets for both pools, and samples.
- A banked reset is spent on an account whose weekly window is at or over its `barFor` bar with a known weekly reset more than three days out (`wantsBankedReset` in `picker.ts`), by `useBankedResets` in `decide.ts`, at the start of every decision without an `enforced` refusal and on the tick. A refusal decision never claims: the claim drops the cached aggregates, and a wall stamped after that is one `landWindows` can never clear, while a claim after the stamp clears it. `Account.bankedReset` is written only through `landGrant` by a direct usage read (`landRead`, codex `applyUsage`, and the `status` merge of a `probe` report, never of a tee fold, which carries no grant and would write back the grant state `status` loaded). The grant has its own clock, `bankedResetAt`, apart from `lastUsageAt`: `landGrant` lands a read's grant only when the read was sent after the clock and then moves the clock to that send time, so a tee that overtakes a read's windows never drops the read's grant, and an older read never overwrites a newer one. `useBankedResets` folds each grant holder's tee (`foldLive`) before the eligibility test, or a stale cached weekly figure spends a grant the tee shows is not needed. Under the pool lock it clears the grant and moves the clock `CLAIM_HOLD_MS` out before the POST, which runs on one engine-owned `AbortSignal` of `CLAIM_DEADLINE_MS`; the hold outlasts the deadline, so neither a read in flight during the claim nor the `status` merge of a record loaded before it restores the spent grant. Every sampler stamps a read with the time it was sent, never the time it returned, or a read in flight during a claim lands as newer and restores the pre-reset figures. A `reset` answer drops the aggregate windows through `p.mergeWindows([], ...)`, leaving the account unmeasured until its next sample, never guessed at 0. The Claude usage read sends `claudeUserAgent()` (`claude-cli/<version> (external, cli)`); without the version the `cedar_ember` block answers `ineligible_reason: "surface"` and no grant is ever seen. Mechanics: the Supermemory documents in the two namespaces `AGENTS.md` names.
- Match model names by the family as a whole token after splitting the id and display name on spaces, dots, and hyphens (`familyTokens` in `usage.ts`), never by the exact display string. Display names drift per release in BOTH directions ("Opus 4.8", and "Fable" became "Fable 5"), so any exact-match gate silently stops matching and an account's cap drains to 100 with no move.
- Unmeasured must never look safe: an unmeasured usage percent renders as unknown, never 0, and ranks last, never first. Unmeasured must never look exhausted either: a failed grok credits read never writes the dollar billing body. An account with no stored sample stays `exhausted` false with `usage` null; one with a stored credits sample keeps that stale figure.
- Per-model weekly caps exist only for Sonnet and Fable, and only Fable gates a switch.
- A session's model is a fact of its process, the same as its seat: the `--model` value in the supervisor's effective flag set (`modelFromFlag` in `usage.ts`, which drops a `[1m]` suffix and returns a model only when the value names a family in `MODEL_FAMILIES`, the binary's alias families). The supervisor builds the `families` of `pickSeat`, the seat watch, and its own `evaluateAndMaybeSwap` call from it and never from a tee, and it sets `TOKENMAXXING_MODEL` on the child so the hooks' `claude.gatedFamilies` reads it ahead of the per-account tee, which reports whichever session on the seat rendered last and does not exist for a harness session. The engine derives `observeLive`'s `perModel` from the same families, so the in-decision per-model sample follows the session's model and never the tee's. A session spawned as Opus gates no family, so a spent Fable cap neither moves it nor screens a candidate for it. `best`, `opusplan`, an unlisted family, and a launch without `--model` are unknown and gate every configured family. The StopFailure classification of a per-model `quotaLimits` row and the blind screen still read `policy.switchModels`: they are the backstop for a `/model` change and for a subagent on another model. A usage-credits refusal classifies whatever that list holds. `MODEL_FAMILIES` is audited on each binary bump (`binary-bump-audit` skill).
